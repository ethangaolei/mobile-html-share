From Outsourced Execution to Cooperation Governance: A Six-Dimension Framework for MNC–CSO Partnerships

CSO partnerships should no longer be treated as a mechanism for outsourcing sales activities. They should be redesigned as a cooperation governance system built around shared objectives, contract architecture, responsibility boundaries, operating cadence, traceable evidence and partner maturity evaluation.

Section 01

1. Core thesis: CSO projects are not sales outsourcing, but cooperation governance

In the new policy, compliance, payment and organizational-efficiency environment, the management question around CSO projects has fundamentally changed. The old keywords were coverage, execution, sales and commission. The new keywords should be goal alignment, contract-led governance, clear boundaries, manageable mechanisms, traceable evidence and evaluable capabilities.

MNCs still need external commercial partners. Internal organizations are leaner; market access and local execution have become more differentiated; and regional coverage, hospital access, channel coordination, patient affordability and customer behavior increasingly require local capabilities. The question is not whether CSOs are needed, but how CSO partnerships should be governed.

Governance does not mean more meetings, more forms or more audits. It means designing the partnership as a system that can be managed, reviewed, held accountable and continuously upgraded.

The future value of a CSO partner is not simply whether it can deliver one year of sales. It is whether it can build long-term capabilities in a defined therapeutic field, patient segment, regional market, access scenario, channel network or customer group.

Section 02

2. The six-dimension governance framework

Shared objectives
Clarify the real commercial problem: incremental growth, white-space coverage, access breakthrough, organizational substitution, portfolio synergy or patient affordability.
Contract design
Move beyond price, commission and duration. Define service scope, standards, data obligations, meetings, compliance responsibility and exit mechanisms.
Responsibility boundaries
Clarify what the MNC leads, what the partner leads, and what must be jointly governed.
Operating cadence
Turn weekly meetings, monthly reviews, JCCs and task-force meetings into a governance rhythm rather than reporting rituals.
Evidence traceability
Build a continuous evidence chain around decisions, services, risks, corrective actions and reviews.
Partner evaluation
Evaluate not only sales and coverage, but also strategy understanding, project management, compliance records, data quality and issue closure.
Section 03

3. Cooperation objectives: from sales targets to a shared commercial thesis

Many CSO projects start by defining a sales target. Sales matter, but sales targets cannot replace cooperation objectives. A proper cooperation objective should answer: why does this product need external partnership? Why this region? Why these hospitals? Why this partner? Success should mean not only revenue, but also the creation of reusable commercial capabilities.

Four layers of objectives
Business: sales, coverage, access, affordability.
Market: customer perception, care pathway, product awareness.
Capability: partner team, data, meetings, issue closure.
Compliance: clear boundaries, traceable process, controllable risks.
Common mistakes
Using historical sales plus growth rate as the goal; treating hospital count as success; mistaking activity volume for capability; treating short-term results as long-term partnership quality.

4. Contract design: from commercial terms to governance architecture

Traditional contracts often overemphasize commercial terms and under-specify governance terms. In the new cycle, the contract should become the legal carrier of governance design, not merely a transaction document.

Objectives and scopeProducts, regions, target hospitals, customers, phase objectives and prohibited activities.Prevent objective drift.

What can be done within an existing contract?

If the current contract still has more than 12 months to run, governance upgrading should not wait for renewal. Start with actions that do not require contract renegotiation: an internal cooperation-objective statement, monthly business reviews, evidence templates, internal service baselines, partner capability observation and an informal review cadence.

5. Responsibility boundaries: from vague collaboration to structured accountability

The most sensitive part of CSO governance is responsibility boundaries. If the MNC goes too deep, it may cross employment, compliance or sales-conduct boundaries. If it stays too loose, strategy distortion, data distortion and delayed risk identification will follow.

Section 06

6. Operating cadence: from reporting meetings to governance rhythm

CSO partnerships do not naturally operate after contract signing. They require continuous calibration. Effective cadence turns deviations, obstacles, capability gaps and risk issues into structured discussions with decisions, owners, timelines and review entry points.

MechanismPurposeRequired outputs
Weekly meetingDrive short-cycle execution and solve immediate obstacles.Actions, owners, deadlines and escalation items.
Monthly business reviewAssess business quality, not only sales.Gap explanation, customer feedback, data quality and corrective actions.
JCC / senior governance meetingResolve boundaries, resources, contracts, capability and major risks.Senior decisions, resource commitments and risk actions.
Section 07

7. Evidence traceability: from retrospective documentation to process records

Evidence traceability is not about keeping as much documentation as possible. It is about keeping evidence that is necessary for governance, explainable for compliance and useful for accountability.

Must retain
Contracts, approval records, service standards, compliance training, activity approvals, payment basis, risk events and remediation records.
Recommended to retain
Review minutes, JCC decisions, action tracking, service deliverables, partner capability assessment and issue-closure records.
Not recommended
Overly granular individual-level records, specific physician feedback, prescription or purchasing intention, unverified customer comments or language that could be misread as sales instruction.
Section 08

8. Partner evaluation: from outcome review to maturity assessment

Partner evaluation should not only ask whether the annual target was achieved. It should ask whether the partner has built long-term cooperation capabilities in a specific field and whether those capabilities can be reused as the product, region, customer and payment environment changes.

DimensionKey questionGovernance implication

Section 10

10. What MNCs must change internally

CSO governance upgrading cannot only demand partner upgrading. The MNC must move from sales supervision to governance architecture.

The internal mechanism should be lightweight but not optional. Daily governance is led by the commercial owner and project PMO; market, medical and access functions should have minimum participation requirements for A-level projects; legal and finance should be involved at key contract, risk and evaluation nodes.

Incentive compatibility

If internal KPIs and bonuses remain almost entirely sales-based, governance indicators will become paper compliance. The project owner’s evaluation should include evidence quality, issue-closure rate, partner maturity improvement and compliance/risk control. A meaningful pilot weight is typically 20–30% for governance-quality indicators, adjusted by project level.

12. Technology infrastructure and AI digital roles

AI governance should not be understood as “deploying tools”. It should be designed as a set of AI digital roles inside the organization. These roles perform stable organizational functions: information processing, rule execution, evidence organization, anomaly detection, draft generation and closure tracking. Human roles retain objectives, judgment, authorization, external commitments and final accountability.

AI agents are digital roles, not tool plugins. AI can enter the organization chart, but it does not become an accountable authority.

Human–AI division of work

AI is responsible for facts, rules, drafts and closure. Humans are responsible for judgment, authorization and accountability. Cross-functional teams define rules, data definitions, professional boundaries and review mechanisms; AI executes, reminds, archives and tracks.

Boundaries

  • AI does not replace business judgment.
  • AI does not replace compliance, legal or medical judgment.
  • AI does not replace people management.
  • AI does not communicate externally on behalf of authorized human roles.
  • AI should avoid unnecessary individual-level sensitive information.
  • AI scoring must be explainable and traceable.

Section 14

14. Conclusion

Under the new policy and compliance cycle, the simple and rough CSO model will become increasingly difficult to sustain. But this does not mean CSOs or external commercialization partnerships will disappear. MNCs will continue to need high-quality external commercial capabilities.

The difference is that future valuable partners will not merely have teams, channels and relationships. They will need project management, compliance records, customer insight, access coordination and issue-closure capabilities. Future capable MNCs will not merely select partners; they will design and operate a cooperation governance system.

The future of CSO projects is not disappearance, but professionalization; not simple outsourcing, but governance upgrading.
