Section 01

1. Core view: CSO is not merely outsourced sales execution

The management question for CSO collaborations has changed. The old keywords were coverage, execution, sales volume and commercial split. The new keywords should be: aligned objectives, contract-led management, clear boundaries, governable mechanisms, traceable evidence and assessable capability.

Multinational pharma companies will still need external commercial partners. Internal organizations are being streamlined, regional market differences are increasing, and access, supply, patient affordability and customer perception increasingly require localized capabilities. The question is not whether CSO partners are still needed. The question is how CSO cooperation should be governed.

Governance does not mean more meetings, more forms or more audits. It means designing the cooperation relationship as a system that can be managed, held accountable, reviewed and continuously upgraded.

Therefore, CSO collaboration should move from project execution management to cooperation governance. MNC pharma companies should no longer only ask: who will sell, how much will they sell, and how will the commercial return be shared? They should first ask: What is the cooperation objective? How does the contract carry governance? Where are the responsibility boundaries? How do both sides interact? How is evidence captured? How will the cooperation be assessed and improved?

More importantly, what MNC pharma companies need in the future is not a partner that simply completes a sales target, but a partner with long-term cooperation capability in specific fields. Such fields may include a therapeutic area, patient segment, regional market, access scenario, channel network or customer type. The value of a partner lies not only in annual sales delivery, but in whether it can continuously contribute local insight, access coordination, customer education, compliant execution, data accumulation and reusable capability.

Section 02

2. The six-dimension cooperation governance framework

A mature CSO cooperation governance system should include six connected dimensions:

Cooperation objectives
Clarify the business problem the cooperation is designed to solve: incremental growth, white-space coverage, access breakthrough, organization substitution, portfolio synergy or patient access improvement.
Contract design
The contract should not only define price, split, term and sales target. It should define quantifiable services, service standards, boundaries, data obligations, meeting mechanisms, compliance duties and exit rules.
Responsibility boundaries
Clarify what is company-led, partner-led and jointly governed. Avoid both overstepping into partner field management and under-governing critical execution.
Interaction rules
Design weekly meetings, monthly reviews, JCC meetings, senior reviews and task-force meetings as governance rhythm, not routine reporting.
Traceable evidence
Build a continuous evidence chain across objectives, contract, process, activities, risks, remediation, results and learning.
Cooperation assessment
Evaluate partners beyond sales and coverage: strategic understanding, project management, district manager capability, compliance records, data quality and issue-closure capability.

Together, these six dimensions determine whether a CSO project is truly governable. Without any one of them, the project easily falls back into a crude model of outsourced sales plus result tracking.

Section 03

3. Cooperation objectives: from sales targets to a shared commercial agenda

Many CSO projects define their objective as a sales target from day one. Sales targets are important, but they cannot replace cooperation objectives. The objective should answer: Why does this product need external cooperation? Why this region? Why these hospitals? Why this partner? Success should mean not only how much is sold, but also what commercial capabilities are being built.

Objectives should be structured into four layers
Business objectives: sales, coverage, access and patient availability.
Market objectives: customer perception, treatment pathway and product understanding.
Capability objectives: partner team, data, meeting and issue-closure capabilities.
Compliance objectives: clear boundaries, process documentation and controllable risk.
Common mistakes
Using last year’s sales plus growth rate as the target; treating the number of hospitals as the target; treating activity volume as the target; treating partner commitment as capability; treating short-term delivery as long-term cooperation quality.

Project initiation should therefore produce a cooperation objective memo: the real business problem, target market logic, portfolio logic, access and channel constraints, rationale for partner selection, and first-stage success criteria.

Section 04

4. Contract design: from commercial terms to governance architecture

Traditional contracts often overemphasize commercial terms and underdefine governance terms. Price, split, duration, territory and sales task may be clear, but service content, service standards, deliverable frequency, process evidence, risk escalation, meeting decisions and exit mechanisms may remain vague.

In the new cycle, the contract should become the legal carrier of governance design, not merely a transaction arrangement. In particular, the contract should avoid being reduced to a sales target. It must specify what quantifiable, verifiable and traceable professional services the partner is expected to provide.

Contract governance moduleWhat should be definedManagement implication
Cooperation objectives and scopeProduct, region, target hospitals, target customers, stage objectives and prohibited activities.Prevents objective drift and keeps execution aligned with the original commercial agenda.
Quantifiable service contentTarget hospital coverage service, customer education and follow-up, market activity organization, access information collection, channel coordination, data reporting and issue feedback. Each service should have frequency, quality standards, deliverables and acceptance criteria.Moves the cooperation from “complete a sales number” to “provide assessable professional commercialization services”.
Responsibilities and boundariesClear lists of company-led, partner-led and jointly governed matters; boundaries against disguised field-force command or overstepping.Reduces employment, compliance and responsibility-mixing risks.

Meeting and escalation mechanismFrequency, participants, topics and action tracking for weekly meetings, monthly reviews, JCC, senior reviews and task-force meetings.Turns interaction rules into an embedded cooperation mechanism rather than personal coordination.
Compliance and audit rightsTraining, approval, activity management, record retention, audit support, violation remediation and responsibility allocation.Embeds risk control into daily project operation.
Assessment and exit mechanismStage assessment, remediation period, capability development plan, termination conditions and transition requirements.Avoids cooperation that can only enter but not exit; also prevents business disruption during exit.
The purpose of contract design is not to push all responsibility to the partner, but to turn cooperation from relationship-driven coordination into mechanism-driven coordination.
Section 05

5. Responsibility boundaries: from vague collaboration to structured division of roles

The most sensitive and failure-prone area in CSO projects is responsibility boundary. If the company manages too deeply, it may cross the boundary of field-force management, sales behavior command and incentive arrangement. If the company manages too loosely, strategy distortion, data distortion and delayed risk identification may follow.

Once boundaries are clear, the company’s management focus is not to replace the partner’s field management, but to manage direction, standards, evidence, risk and review.

Section 06

6. Interaction rules: from reporting meetings to governance rhythm

CSO cooperation does not run naturally after the contract is signed. It needs continuous calibration. Interaction rules allow both sides to handle objective deviation, execution barriers, capability gaps and risk issues through a fixed rhythm.

Interaction mechanismCore purposeRequired output
Weekly meetingDrive short-cycle execution and remove immediate barriers in key hospitals, customer follow-up, supply and access.This week’s actions, owners, deadlines and issues requiring escalation.
Monthly business reviewReview business quality, not only sales.Target gap explanation, customer feedback, activity quality, district manager capability, data quality and corrective actions.
JCC / senior governance meetingResolve boundary, resource, contract, capability and major risk issues.Senior decisions, resource commitments, contract adjustment and major risk handling.
Task-force meetingResolve cross-functional or cross-organization issues such as access, supply, compliance remediation or key hospital breakthroughs.Task plan, milestones and cross-functional responsibilities.
Annual / semi-annual cooperation assessmentDecide whether to continue, adjust, upgrade or exit the cooperation.Partner maturity assessment, cooperation value assessment and next-stage cooperation design.
The mark of effective interaction is not more meetings. It is whether every interaction leaves clear decisions, responsibilities, timelines and review points.
Section 07

7. Traceable evidence: from after-the-fact documentation to process evidence

In the new policy cycle, CSO projects must produce traceable evidence. Evidence is not a documentation burden. It answers three questions: Why was this decision made? What happened during the process? How were issues corrected?

Objective evidence
Rationale and approvals for product, region, hospital list, targets and partner selection.
Contract evidence
Contract terms, supplemental agreements, meeting mechanisms, data obligations, compliance responsibilities and exit conditions.
Process evidence
Meeting minutes, action tracking, customer feedback, activity review, access progress, supply and channel issues.
Capability evidence
Project lead, district manager, field team, data quality and issue-closure capability.
Compliance evidence
Training, approval, activity execution, audit, exception events, remediation and contract obligation performance.
Assessment evidence
Stage results, cooperation value, capability changes, risk record and renewal or exit rationale.

If a CSO project only has sales results but lacks evidence on objectives, contract, process, compliance and assessment, it cannot be considered governance-mature.

Section 08

8. Cooperation assessment: from result review to partner maturity evaluation

Partner assessment used to focus on three things: sales, coverage and cooperation attitude. These remain important, but they are not sufficient to judge whether a partner is suitable for long-term cooperation. Future assessment should move from result review to maturity evaluation.

Assessment dimensionKey questionGovernance implication
Strategic understandingDoes the partner understand product positioning, target patients, customer perception and market strategy?Determines whether strategy can be translated correctly in the field.
Project managementCan the partner turn objectives into plans, actions, owners and timelines?Determines whether commitment becomes execution.
District manager capabilityCan district managers coach representatives, identify opportunities, solve problems and manage key hospitals?Determines regional execution quality.
Customer insightCan the partner provide real, specific and verifiable customer feedback?Determines whether the company can adjust strategy in time.
Access and channel coordinationCan the partner manage regional access, channel supply, local policy and field barriers?Determines whether objectives have real conditions for delivery.
Compliance and documentationDoes the partner understand boundaries and maintain true, timely and complete records?Determines whether risk is controllable and process is auditable.
Issue closureCan the partner continuously drive issue identification, analysis, action and review?Determines whether the partner has continuous improvement capability.

The result of assessment should not only be “renew or not renew”. It should lead to three management actions: partner capability development plan, cooperation scope adjustment, and exit or replacement plan. The core question is not simply whether this year’s target was achieved, but whether the partner has accumulated long-term cooperation capability in a specific field and whether such capability can be reused as products, regions, customers and payment conditions change.

Section 09

9. What MNC pharma companies must change internally

CSO governance upgrade cannot only ask partners to improve. If MNC pharma companies still manage external cooperation with traditional sales-management logic, two risks emerge: overstepping into partner field management and creating compliance/legal risks, or focusing only on outcomes while giving up process governance.

1. Management approach: from sales supervision to governance architecture

Old approach
Push sales, chase progress, check activities, pressure targets and rely on personal relationships to solve problems. Management is short-term, field-level and result-driven.
New approach
Design objectives, contracts, boundaries, interaction rules, evidence and assessment mechanisms. Management is systematic, mechanism-based and review-oriented.

The project owner in an MNC pharma company should move from being a business supervisor of an external sales team to the owner of a cooperation governance system. They should not directly command the partner’s representatives on how to visit customers, but they must ensure that objectives are correct, boundaries are clear, data is reliable, risk is escalated and issues are closed.

2. Internal organizational responsibilities: from single-function management to cross-functional governance

CSO projects should not sit only with sales or commercial teams. Mature governance requires redefined responsibilities across functions:

Internal functionNew responsibility focus
Commercial / sales managementDefine business objectives, target hospital logic, execution rhythm, partner capability requirements and business review mechanism.
Marketing / productDefine product positioning, customer segmentation, core messages, activity strategy and customer perception assessment.
MedicalEnsure medical information boundaries, evidence support, academic activity quality and compliant use of medical resources.
Access / government affairs / commercial insuranceManage local policy, access pathway, payment conditions, commercial insurance coordination and key external barriers.

Finance / procurementManage payment logic, performance conditions, contract fulfillment, fee reasonableness and vendor-management rules.

This means MNC pharma companies should establish a CSO cooperation governance group or commercial cooperation governance mechanism rather than leaving the project to one sales owner.

3. People requirements: from managing sales to managing a cooperation system

People responsible for CSO projects will need a different capability mix:

Commercial judgment
Assess whether the cooperation objective is real, targets are reasonable and regional opportunities are valid.
Contract and boundary awareness
Understand contract terms, compliance boundaries, partner responsibilities and management red lines.
Project governance capability
Design meeting rhythm, action tracking, issue escalation and stage review instead of only chasing progress.
Cross-functional coordination
Mobilize marketing, medical, access, compliance, finance and supply-chain resources to solve problems.
Data and evidence mindset
Judge business quality through data and records, not only oral updates and regional impressions.
Partner development capability
Identify partner gaps, drive capability development, and recommend replacement or exit when necessary.
The biggest shift for MNC pharma companies is from “how do I use a partner to complete sales” to “how do I design a sustainable, auditable and upgradeable external commercialization capability system”.
Section 10

10. Closing thought: governance means bringing external capability into a controllable system

In the new policy cycle, crude CSO models will become increasingly difficult to sustain. But this does not mean CSO or commercial cooperation models will disappear. On the contrary, as organizations become leaner, markets become more regionalized, payment becomes more diversified and local differences increase, MNC pharma companies will need high-quality external commercial capabilities even more.

The difference is that future valuable partners will not simply be those with field teams, channels or relationships. They must have project management, compliance records, customer insight, access coordination and issue-closure capabilities. More precisely, what MNC pharma companies need is not a partner that only completes a sales target, but a partner with long-term cooperation capability in specific fields.

The future of CSO projects is not disappearance, but professionalization; not simple outsourcing, but cooperation governance upgrade.
