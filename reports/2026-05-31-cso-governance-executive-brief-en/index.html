From Outsourced Execution to Partner Governance

Rethinking CSO Project Management for Multinational Pharma in a New Policy Cycle

MNC PharmaCSO ProjectsPartner GovernanceCompliance Boundary

Executive Summary

In the new policy cycle, CSO projects should no longer be managed merely as outsourced sales execution. For multinational pharmaceutical companies, the management priority is to build a partner governance system with clear boundaries, defined responsibilities, process visibility, compliance control, and reviewable evidence.

The core question is no longer “who can sell for us?” but “how do we govern an external commercial network within clear compliance and management boundaries?”

1. Why the Model Needs to Change

Traditional CSO collaborations were built on a relatively simple logic: the pharmaceutical company provided product strategy, brand, medical and marketing direction, while the commercial partner provided regional coverage, field execution, channel coordination and customer access.

This model worked when growth was strong, compliance boundaries were less explicit, and internal resources were more available. Today, the environment has changed. Anti-corruption enforcement, compliance scrutiny, volume-based procurement, payer pressure, commercial insurance pilots, organizational cost discipline and regional market complexity are reshaping commercialization.

2. Main Weaknesses of the Old CSO Management Approach

Unclear objectives
Product strategy, access, sales, medical support and market activities are not always aligned around one project logic.
Weak target selection
Target hospitals and opportunity lists are often changed without sufficient evidence.
Historical target setting
Sales targets are overly based on past sales rather than potential, access status, payer conditions and competition.
Partner capability gaps
Coverage and past sales do not necessarily indicate project leadership, compliance, data or market capability.
Strategy-execution disconnect
Brand strategy may be diluted into activity volume or short-term sales actions in the field.
Meetings without closure
Too many meetings track updates, but not enough decisions, owners, timelines and issue closure.

3. Upgrade One: Clarify Responsibility Boundaries

MNC pharma companies should lead product strategy, medical and market positioning, target patient and customer definition, core messages, compliance framework, contract governance, data standards, JCC mechanisms and risk escalation.

CSO partners should lead field team management, district execution, daily customer engagement, local coordination and operational rhythm.

Joint governance is required for target hospital selection, customer segmentation, access objectives, market activity quality, supply and channel issues, performance review and compliance risk monitoring.

4. Upgrade Two: Build Full-Cycle Partner Governance

A CSO project should not be managed only at launch or through routine reports. It should be governed across six modules:

  • Partnership setup: business purpose, target market, partner rationale, hospital list, governance terms.
  • Team capability: project lead commitment, district manager quality, field team capability, compliance awareness.
  • Supply and access: forecast accuracy, access objectives, delisting/removal restrictions, channel issues.
  • Sales strategy and execution: customer segmentation, opportunity tracking, field record quality, medical support.
  • Market activities: strategic fit, target customer relevance, post-event follow-up and impact review.
  • Operational excellence and compliance: data visibility, KPI tracking, audit readiness, risk escalation.

5. Upgrade Three: Turn Meetings into a Management System

Weekly meetings should drive execution. Monthly meetings should review business quality. JCC meetings should address governance, boundary and resource issues. Special-purpose meetings should close cross-functional problems such as access, supply, compliance remediation, key hospital breakthroughs or data quality issues.

A useful meeting must answer four questions: What was decided last time? What problem are we solving today? Who owns the next step? When will we review progress?

6. Upgrade Four: Create Reviewable Management Evidence

CSO governance requires evidence across six areas:

  • Objective evidence: why this product, region, hospital list, target and partner were selected.
  • Process evidence: meeting minutes, action tracking, customer feedback, activity reviews and access progress.
  • Capability evidence: project lead, district manager, field execution, data quality and issue-closure capability.
  • Compliance evidence: training records, activity approval, audit records, incidents, remediation and contract obligations.
  • Result evidence: sales, access, hospital coverage, customer perception change and project milestones.
  • Learning evidence: what worked, what failed, what is repeatable, and what should be adjusted next.

7. Upgrade Five: Redefine Partner Evaluation

Partner evaluation should go beyond sales outcomes, coverage and cooperation attitude. It should assess strategic understanding, project management capability, district manager quality, customer insight, access and channel coordination, market activity execution, compliance and documentation discipline, and issue-closure capability.

Implication for MNC Pharma

The future of CSO projects is not disappearance, but governance upgrade. High-value CSO partners will become professional commercialization infrastructure rather than a simple extension of the sales force.

Capable MNC pharma companies will not merely select partners; they will design and operate a partner governance system.
